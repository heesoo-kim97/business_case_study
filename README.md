# Business Case Analysis

A collection of **real-world business case studies** examining how companies make strategic and operational decisions across industries.

The purpose of this repository is to develop and demonstrate the ability to:

**Understand a business → Research its strategy → Analyze evidence → Identify opportunities and risks → Develop recommendations**

Cases focus primarily on **business strategy, operations, supply chain, and technology**, with an emphasis on understanding how different parts of a business connect.

---

## Purpose

Business decisions rarely exist in isolation.

A company's supply chain strategy can affect inventory.
Inventory decisions can affect customer experience.
Customer experience can affect revenue.
Technology can enable or constrain operations.

These cases investigate those connections using publicly available information such as:

* Annual reports
* SEC filings
* Investor presentations
* Earnings reports
* Company websites
* Company statements
* Industry reports
* Government publications
* Academic and research sources
* Reputable news and business publications

The goal is not simply to summarize what a company does, but to understand **why the company may be pursuing a particular strategy and what could be done next.**

# Case Study Framework

Each case follows a structured business-analysis process.

### 1. Company & Industry Context

* What does the company do?
* How does it make money?
* What industry does it operate in?
* Who are its customers?
* What are its major products/services?
* What competitive and operational factors influence the business?

### 2. Business Problem / Strategic Question

Identify a specific business question.

Examples:

* How should the company improve supply-chain resilience?
* How should the company balance cost and customer service?
* Should the company expand its distribution network?
* How should the company respond to changing customer demand?
* How can the company reduce operational risk?
* How should the company approach AI adoption?

### 3. Current Strategy

Examine the company's publicly stated strategy.

What is the company actually trying to accomplish?

Consider:

* Growth strategy
* Cost strategy
* Supply-chain strategy
* Procurement strategy
* Operations strategy
* Technology strategy
* Customer strategy
* Geographic expansion
* Vertical integration
* Partnerships
* Capital investment

### 4. Evidence & Research

Use publicly available information to investigate the strategy.

Rather than relying on a single source, compare evidence across:

* Financial performance
* Management statements
* Capital investments
* Facility expansion
* Supplier relationships
* Acquisitions
* Partnerships
* Product launches
* Geographic expansion
* Operational changes
* Industry conditions

### 5. Strategic Analysis

Analyze the relationship between the company's strategy and its business environment.

Questions may include:

* What problem is the strategy attempting to solve?
* What advantages does the strategy create?
* What tradeoffs does it introduce?
* What risks could prevent success?
* What assumptions does the strategy depend on?
* How does the strategy compare with industry dynamics?

### 6. Opportunities & Risks

Identify potential areas where the company could:

* Improve efficiency
* Reduce costs
* Increase resilience
* Improve customer experience
* Expand capacity
* Reduce operational risk
* Improve visibility
* Adopt new technology
* Enter new markets
* Strengthen competitive advantages

### 7. Recommendations

Develop potential actions based on the evidence gathered.

Recommendations should explain:

**What should change → Why → How it could work → Potential benefits → Potential risks**

Recommendations are analytical opinions based on the available evidence and are not intended to represent the company's actual plans.

### 8. Implementation Considerations

Consider what would be required to execute the recommendation:

* People
* Processes
* Technology
* Capital
* Suppliers
* Partners
* Organizational changes
* Timeline
* Risks
* KPIs

### 9. Success Measures

Identify how the company could determine whether the strategy is working.

Potential measures include:

* Revenue
* Cost
* Margin
* Inventory turnover
* On-time delivery
* Lead time
* Capacity utilization
* Customer satisfaction
* Service levels
* Supply-chain resilience
* Employee productivity

# Research → Strategy → Recommendation

The central framework of this repository is:

**Research**

What does the evidence tell us?

↓

**Understand**

What is the company trying to accomplish?

↓

**Analyze**

Why is the company pursuing this strategy?

↓

**Evaluate**

What opportunities, risks, and tradeoffs exist?

↓

**Recommend**

What could the company do next?

↓

**Measure**

How would we know whether it worked?

# Future Technical Analysis

Technical analysis is intentionally separated from the initial business case.

After developing the business case, a future stage may investigate:

* What data would be required?
* What KPIs should be tracked?
* What SQL analysis could test the hypothesis?
* What Python models could support the analysis?
* What dashboards could monitor performance?
* What systems or technology could enable the recommendation?

This creates a progression from:

**Business Problem → Strategy → Analytical Question → Data → Technical Solution**

## Disclaimer

All cases are created for **educational, analytical, and portfolio purposes**.

Unless otherwise stated, research is based on **publicly available information**. No confidential, proprietary, or non-public company information is used.

Company strategies, interpretations, and recommendations presented in these cases represent independent analysis and should not be interpreted as official statements or recommendations from the companies discussed.

Where assumptions are made, they will be clearly identified.
